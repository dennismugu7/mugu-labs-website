For online sellers, the end of the year is the best of times and the most chaotic of times. Orders jump, customers want everything yesterday, riders are stretched, and you're packing parcels at midnight.

The sellers who enjoy the season, rather than just survive it, have one thing in common: they got ready in November.

## The dates that matter this year

- **Friday 27 November: Black Friday.** Many shoppers now expect deals online, and plenty start looking the week before.
- **Friday 25 December: Christmas.** Gift orders peak in the two weeks before.
- **Monday 4 January: schools reopen.** Uniforms, bags and shoes are on every parent's list from late December.

## Your festive timeline

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Now to mid-November** | Pick your bestsellers and restock them. Plan two or three gift bundles. Take fresh product photos. |
| **Mid-November to Black Friday** | Announce your deals early. Save quick replies. Agree delivery days and prices with your riders. |
| **1 to 20 December** | Peak gift weeks. Pack daily, confirm payments before sending, and post your Christmas delivery cut-off date. |
| **21 to 31 December** | Slow down deliveries, thank your customers, and set aside money for January stock. |
| **Early January** | Push back-to-school items and anything left over. |

## 1. Stock what actually sells

Look back at your last few months. Which five items sold most, and which sold out? Restock those first. The festive season is not the time to experiment with ten new products; it's the time to have your proven winners in every size and colour.

## 2. Make gifting easy

Shoppers in December are often buying for someone else. Help them:

- **Bundles**: a dress with matching earrings, a tote with a purse, a "gift set" at a round price.
- **Gift wrapping**: even simple paper and a ribbon can justify a small extra charge.
- **Gift notes**: offer to include a short handwritten message.

## 3. Sort delivery before it sorts you

Delivery is where festive orders go wrong. Before the rush:

- **Agree with your riders** which days they'll work and at what price, especially around the 24th and 31st.
- **Publish a cut-off date** for Christmas delivery, such as *"Order by 20 December for delivery before Christmas"*, and stick to it.
- **Be clear on fees** by area, so customers aren't surprised at the end.

## 4. Protect yourself on payment

Busy seasons attract scammers, who count on you being rushed. Keep your routine:

- Confirm every payment in your **own** M-Pesa messages or statement before sending. A screenshot is not proof.
- For pay-on-delivery orders from new customers, a **small deposit** is fair to both sides.
- Ignore "I sent it by mistake, please send it back" messages. Real reversals go through Safaricom, not through you.

## 5. Get your replies ready

When orders double, so do the questions. Save quick replies for prices, delivery areas, cut-off dates and payment instructions. Answer in batches at set times, not every minute.

## 6. Pay future you

It's easy to spend December's takings in December. Before you celebrate, set aside money for **January restocking**, plus any delivery and supplier bills still to come. January sales can be slow, and you'll want stock ready when they pick up.

## Your festive checklist

- [ ] Bestsellers restocked
- [ ] Two or three gift bundles ready
- [ ] Fresh photos taken
- [ ] Riders booked and delivery prices agreed
- [ ] Christmas delivery cut-off date posted
- [ ] Quick replies saved
- [ ] January restock money set aside
