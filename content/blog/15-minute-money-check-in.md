Most of us don't avoid our finances because we're careless. We avoid them because looking feels bad. You open your statement, see a number you didn't expect, close it, and promise yourself you'll "sort it out properly" later. Later never comes.

So here's a smaller promise: fifteen minutes, once a month. Not a budget overhaul. Not a new life. Just a check-in.

## Why monthly, and why now

Prices have been climbing. The Kenya National Bureau of Statistics put September 2026 inflation at 6.8%, the highest in a year, with food up 9.5% and transport up 15.6% compared with a year earlier. When everyday costs move that fast, last month's version of you was budgeting for a different reality. A monthly check-in is how you catch up before the gap gets wide.

## The check-in, step by step

**Minutes 1–3: Get the numbers in one place.**
Download last month's statement. Most banks let you export it from online banking, often as a CSV or Excel file. For M-Pesa, you can request a free statement through *334# or the Safaricom app. It arrives as a password-protected PDF.

**Minutes 4–8: Sort, don't judge.**
Group what went out into a handful of buckets: rent, food, transport, bills, family, everything else. Five or six buckets is enough. You're not auditing yourself, just drawing a rough map.

**Minutes 9–11: Find one surprise.**
Look for the bucket that's bigger than you thought. There's almost always one. Maybe it's transport, maybe it's the small daily spends that never feel like spending. Circle it. That's this month's whole lesson.

**Minutes 12–15: Make one small decision.**
Not five. One. "I'll carry lunch on Mondays and Wednesdays." "I'll set aside the school-fees money the day salary lands." Write it down where you'll see it.

That's it. Close the laptop.

## What makes it stick

- **Same day, every month.** Tie it to something that already happens, like the weekend after payday.
- **Keep it boring.** The goal isn't a perfect picture. It's a picture at all.
- **Be kind to last month.** You can't change it. You can only learn from it.
