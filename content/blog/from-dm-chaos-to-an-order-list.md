If you sell online in Kenya, you know the scene. A customer asks the price on TikTok. Another sends "size M, same as last time" on WhatsApp. Someone else pays and sends a screenshot on Instagram. By evening you're scrolling through three apps trying to remember who ordered what, who has paid, and whose parcel is still on the table.

None of this means you're disorganised. It means your shop has outgrown your inbox. The good news: you don't need expensive software to fix it. You need one list and a few habits.

## Step 1: Give every order a number

The moment someone confirms, give them an order number: #101, #102, #103. Tell the customer too: *"Thanks! Your order number is #104."*

It sounds small, but it changes everything. You stop saying "the lady with the green dress" and start saying "#104". Customers can ask about their order without re-explaining it, and nothing gets mixed up.

## Step 2: Keep one order list

Every order goes into **one** place, wherever it came from. A notebook works. A simple spreadsheet works better, because you can search and sort it.

| Order | Customer | Phone | Items | Total (KES) | Paid? | Delivery | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| #104 | Wanjiku | 07XX… | Ankara dress (M) | 2,700 | Yes | Westlands | Packed |
| #105 | Brian | 07XX… | Tote bag | 1,800 | Deposit | Kilimani | New |

The rule: **if it's not on the list, it's not an order.** No exceptions, even for friends.

## Step 3: Use the same five stages for every order

Move each order through the same stages, in order:

1. **New**: the customer has confirmed what they want.
2. **Paid**: you've confirmed the money in *your own* M-Pesa messages or statement. A screenshot alone doesn't count.
3. **Packed**: it's wrapped, labelled with the order number, and ready.
4. **Sent**: it's with your rider or courier.
5. **Delivered**: the customer has it.

At any moment you can look at the list and see exactly what needs doing next.

## Step 4: Check messages at set times

Replying the instant every message arrives keeps you busy all day and organised never. Instead, pick two or three set times, say 9am, 1pm and 6pm, to go through every app, add new orders to the list and update statuses. Customers quickly learn when you reply, and you get your day back.

## Step 5: Let your phone do the typing

If you use WhatsApp Business, its **quick replies**, **labels** and **catalogue** save a lot of typing. Save replies for prices, delivery areas and fees, and payment instructions. Use labels to mark chats as *New order*, *Waiting for payment* or *To deliver*.

A few saved replies to start with:

- *"Thanks for your order! Your order number is #___. Total is KES ___ including delivery to ___."*
- *"We deliver within Nairobi the next day. Delivery is KES ___ to your area."*
- *"Payment received, thank you! We'll send your order on ___."*

## Step 6: Close the day properly

Before you sleep, spend five minutes on the list. Is anything paid but not packed? Packed but not sent? Sent but not confirmed delivered? Those are tomorrow's first jobs.
