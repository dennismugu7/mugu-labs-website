Mugu Labs began with frustration. Software that was meant to make life easier kept doing the opposite: too many screens, too many settings, too many features nobody asked for. So Dennis started building his own tools, and somewhere along the way it became a small studio.

Here's what we've learned about how we like to build.

## We use the thing first

Every product starts with a real person sitting next to us while we build it: a salon owner, a seller, someone tired of their own spreadsheet. We watch where they hesitate, what they skip and what they ask for twice. If we wouldn't use it ourselves, and they wouldn't miss it if it disappeared, it isn't ready.

## Small, then smaller

It's tempting to add one more feature. Then another. Before long an app does twenty things badly instead of one thing well. We pick one real problem, solve it properly, and then ask what we can take away. A feature has to earn its place.

## Built to be left alone

The best apps are the ones you forget about because they just work. They don't need you to babysit them, and they run happily on a five-year-old Android with a tired battery and patchy data. A lot of people here use phones like that, and they deserve software that respects it.

## Long after launch

Shipping is the start, not the finish line. We keep listening, fixing and refining for as long as people use what we've made. If something breaks or confuses you, we want to hear about it. A real person reads every message.
