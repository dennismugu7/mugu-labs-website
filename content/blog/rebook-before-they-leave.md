Picture the best moment of any appointment. The cape comes off, your client turns to the mirror, and they smile. They feel good, they trust you, and they're already thinking about how long this look will last.

That's the moment to book their next visit. Not next month, when they're scrolling for a stylist. Right now, while they're still in your chair.

## Why this one habit matters so much

A client who leaves with a next date already in their calendar is far more likely to come back to you than one who leaves with "I'll call you". Life gets busy, a friend recommends someone else, and "I'll call you" quietly becomes "I went somewhere closer".

Rebooking also helps you. You can see your coming weeks filling up, plan your stock and staff, and spot quiet days early enough to do something about them.

## How to ask without it feeling like selling

**Make it about their hair, not your diary.**
Instead of "Do you want to book again?", try: *"To keep these braids looking fresh, I'd see you again in about six weeks. Shall we find a date now?"* You're giving advice, not making a sales pitch.

**Know the right gap for each service.**
Be ready with a natural interval: a trim every six to eight weeks, a relaxer touch-up every eight to ten, braids redone after six to eight. Your experience is the expert here; adjust to what works for your clients' hair and budgets.

**Offer two options, not an open question.**
*"I have Saturday the 14th at 10, or Tuesday the 17th after work. Which suits you?"* Two clear choices are easier to say yes to than "When would you like to come?"

**Ask everyone, every time.**
Make it part of finishing the appointment, like brushing off the cape. Clients stop noticing it as a "pitch" when it's simply how your salon works.

**Confirm it in writing.**
Send a quick message with the date, time and service before they've reached the door. People keep appointments they can see.

## If they say "I'm not sure yet"

That's fine. Don't push. Instead:

- *"No problem. I'll send you a reminder in five weeks so you can pick a date."*
- Then actually send it.

A friendly nudge at the right time often works better than a booking made under pressure.

## A small reward for planners

Some salons give a little thank-you to clients who rebook on the spot: a free treatment add-on, priority on Saturday slots, or a small discount. It doesn't need to be big. It just makes saying yes feel good.

## Your rebooking routine, in five lines

1. Finish the service and let them enjoy the mirror moment.
2. Recommend when they should come back, in terms of their hair.
3. Offer two specific dates.
4. Send a written confirmation straight away.
5. If they're not ready, promise a reminder, and send it.
