The message usually starts the same way. *"Habari yako? Kindly assist…"*

It might be school fees for a nephew, a hospital bill, a harambee, or simply "something small for upkeep". Most of us want to help. Family is often the reason we got where we are. But when requests arrive faster than salary, love and pressure start to feel like the same thing.

You're far from alone. A 2025 national survey by the Central Bank of Kenya, KNBS and FSD Kenya counted more than 10.7 million people receiving money from others in a single year. Supporting each other is woven into how we live. The goal of this post isn't to give less. It's to give in a way you can keep doing.

## 1. Give your family a line in the budget

The biggest change is also the simplest: treat family support like rent. Decide a monthly amount you can sustain, set it aside on payday, and give from that.

There's no right number. It depends on your income, your own dependants and your goals. Start with what you've actually been sending over the last three months. That's your real number today. Then decide whether it's sustainable.

## 2. Sort requests into three kinds

Not every request is the same, and treating them all as emergencies is what drains people.

| Kind | Examples | How to handle it |
|---|---|---|
| **Commitments** | A parent's monthly upkeep, a sibling's school fees | Fixed amount, same day each month, from your family line |
| **Emergencies** | Hospital, a funeral, urgent repairs | From an emergency buffer you've built for exactly this |
| **Requests** | A new phone, a celebration, "a small loan" | Only if the family line has room this month |

Once you know which kind a request is, the answer becomes much easier.

## 3. Pay on a schedule

When support arrives on the same date every month, everyone can plan around it, and the "can you send something today?" messages tend to slow down. Predictable giving feels more generous than scattered giving, even when the total is the same.

## 4. Build a buffer for real emergencies

Emergencies are what push people into quick mobile loans that cost far more later. A small, separate emergency fund, even a few thousand shillings to start, means you can say yes to a real crisis without borrowing.

## 5. Pay the bill, not the person, when you can

Where it makes sense, pay the school's paybill, the hospital or the landlord directly. It's simpler for everyone, avoids misunderstandings, and you'll have a clear record of what you've contributed.

## 6. Share the load

If you're one of several siblings, a family group with a shared monthly contribution, a bit like a chama, can be fairer than one person carrying everything. Agree who contributes what, and keep the records visible.

## 7. Saying "not now", kindly

You can say no and still say it with love. A few lines that help:

- *"I've already sent this month's family support. I can help again on the first."*
- *"I can't send cash right now, but I can pay the fee directly to the school."*
- *"I can manage 2,000 this time, not 10,000. I hope it helps."*

Honesty early is kinder than silence, or a yes you can't afford.

## Look after yourself too

Constant requests can weigh on your mind as much as your wallet. If it's affecting your sleep, your health or your relationships, talk to someone you trust. Protecting your own stability isn't selfish. It's what lets you keep showing up for the people you love.
