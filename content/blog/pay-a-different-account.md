Buying and selling through WhatsApp, TikTok and Instagram feels personal. You chat with a real person, see real photos, agree a price. That warmth is exactly why it works, and exactly what scammers lean on.

The good news: almost every common trick falls apart against a few simple habits.

## If you're buying

**Be suspicious of a sudden new payment number.**
"Our till is down, please send to this number instead" is one of the oldest lines there is. If a shop's payment details change mid-chat, stop and confirm through a channel you already trust, like a call to a number you've used before.

**Read the name before you enter your PIN.**
When you pay with M-Pesa, the confirmation shows who will receive the money. Check that it matches the shop you think you're paying. If it's a different person's name, cancel. That one second is the cheapest protection you have.

**For a new seller, prefer pay-on-delivery or a small deposit.**
There's a reason cash-on-delivery stays popular here: a DHL e-commerce report found nearly three in four Kenyan online shoppers prefer to pay when the goods arrive. For a first order with someone new, paying part now and the rest at your door is fair to both sides.

**Look for real history.**
Reviews and photos from actual buyers, delivery stories, a shop that's been around a while. Brand-new pages with prices far below everyone else's deserve extra caution.

## If you're selling

**Never ship on a screenshot.**
Screenshots are easy to fake. Before you hand anything over, check your own M-Pesa messages or balance for the payment. If it isn't there, it hasn't arrived.

**Watch for "I sent it by mistake, please send it back."**
A message claiming someone sent you money by accident, often with a sad story attached, is a classic trick. The "payment" message is usually fake. Don't send anything. If money really was sent in error, the sender can ask Safaricom to reverse it; you don't need to do it yourself.

**Know the official channels.**
Safaricom advises forwarding fake M-Pesa messages to **333**, and wrong-transaction reversals go through **456**. Safaricom also says its staff will never ask you to visit an agent to process a reversal. Anyone who does is not Safaricom.

**Keep your PIN to yourself, always.**
No real customer, courier, agent or "customer care" caller ever needs your M-Pesa PIN or a one-time code.

## Trust is a habit

None of this means being cold to buyers or sellers. It means having a few routines you follow every time, so you don't have to rely on gut feeling when someone is rushing you. Scams need urgency to work. Routines take the urgency away.
