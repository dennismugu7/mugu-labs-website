Think of a client who used to come every six weeks like clockwork. Then one month she didn't. Then another. You wondered if she was unhappy, felt a bit awkward about it, and said nothing.

Here's the thing: most quiet clients haven't gone anywhere on purpose. They got busy, money was tight one month, or they simply forgot. Many are one friendly message away from booking again.

## Step 1: Find your quiet regulars

A "quiet regular" is someone who used to visit regularly and has now missed their usual return time by a few weeks. If a client normally comes every six weeks and it's been ten, they're quiet.

Go through your bookings book, your phone or your appointment app and list them. Ten names is a great start.

## Step 2: Write like a person, not a promotion

The message that works best sounds like you, not like an advert. Keep it short, warm and personal. Use their name, mention their usual service, and make booking easy.

### Template: the friendly check-in

> Hi **[Name]**! It's **[your name]** from **[salon]**. It's been a little while since your last **[service]**, and we've missed you. If you'd like to come in, I have **[day]** at **[time]** or **[day]** at **[time]** this week. Just reply and I'll hold one for you.

### Template: the small welcome back

> Hi **[Name]**, hope you're keeping well! We'd love to see you again, so your next **[service]** comes with a free **[small add-on, e.g. hot-oil treatment]** this month. Reply with a day that suits you and I'll book you in.

### Template: the "something new" message

> Hi **[Name]**! We've just started offering **[new service or product]** and I thought of you. Would you like to try it at your next visit? I have a few slots open on **[day]**.

## Step 3: Send it at a good time

- **Weekday evenings** tend to be when people have a moment to reply.
- **Avoid month-end crunch** if your clients are salaried; just after payday is often kinder.
- **Message individually,** never in a big broadcast group. Nobody wants their number shared with strangers.

## Step 4: Follow up once, then let it be

If there's no reply after a week, one gentle follow-up is fine: *"Just checking you saw this, no pressure at all!"* After that, leave it. Respecting people's space keeps your salon's reputation warm, even with clients who don't come back right away.

## Step 5: When they do come back

Make their return feel noticed. Greet them by name, remember their usual style, and before they leave, book their next visit while they're still in the chair. That's how a quiet regular becomes a regular again.

## A few things to avoid

- **Guilt.** "Why have you abandoned us?" makes people avoid you.
- **Long messages.** If it needs scrolling, it's too long.
- **Messaging people who asked you not to.** Always respect a "please stop".
